What a Swim Lesson for Babies and Toddlers Looks Like

  • November 17, 2026
  • Safe Swimming School
  • Swim Tips

Parents often ask us the same thing before the first lesson: what actually happens in the water? Here is a plain walk through it, so you know what to expect at your own pool.

Lessons happen at your pool

We come to you. Lessons take place at your home pool or a community pool in Palm Beach County, between 8 AM and 7 PM, every day of the week. Your child learns in familiar water, and you do not have to pack up and drive anywhere.

How the first lessons start

Each lesson is 30 minutes and private. Babies as young as 3 months can start. For infants, the first lessons begin with a parent in the water, then move to private lessons with the instructor. Our certified instructors go at your swimmer's pace, so a nervous toddler is never rushed.

The skills we teach

Our survival swim lessons focus on a short list of real skills, taught in small steps:

  • Getting comfortable with water on the face
  • Rolling from the front onto the back
  • Floating and resting on the back (we say it a lot: you have to be flat to float)
  • Kicking and turning toward the wall or steps
  • Holding on to the wall and climbing out

Skills come first. Strokes come later, after the safety skills are in place.

What parents do

Plan to be at the pool and watching. You can ask questions, see each step, and practice the same cues between lessons. Siblings can share a package and swim in their own turns, one swimmer at a time.

What to bring

  • A swim diaper and a snug swimsuit for babies
  • A towel and a warm layer for after
  • A full tummy, but not too full

A note on supervision

Swim skills are one layer of protection. Nothing replaces an adult watching the water, and pool barriers and gates still matter. Keep a grown up within arm's reach of little swimmers, every time.
